1. What did I accomplish this week?

This week was the pond plants research. No new pages yet, but all the content and images that the plants pages will need.

- Picked the species. Twenty plants: six floating (water lily, lotus, water hyacinth, water lettuce, duckweed, frogbit), four underwater (hornwort, anacharis, eelgrass, Anubias) and ten for the edge of the pond (pickerel rush, water iris, marsh marigold, sweet flag, umbrella sedge, taro, watercress, water mint, raupō and creeping Jenny). I chose them to cover what a koi keeper actually goes shopping for, not just what is easy to write about.

- Researched each one properly. Every plant has two sources for the general facts (the RHS, university extension pages, botanical gardens, koi farms) plus a New Zealand source for its legal status. For each plant I wrote down: what type it is, how much light it wants, how hard it is, whether koi will eat it and how to stop them, how cold it can go, how deep to plant it and how big it gets, what it does for the pond, and a short plain description.

- Found out that a lot of the classic pond plants are banned here. Water hyacinth, water lettuce, hornwort, anacharis and eelgrass are all on New Zealand's National Pest Plant Accord, so they cannot be sold or moved. Yellow flag iris and the northern bulrush are banned lookalikes of two plants I do include. Our duckweed turns out to be a native species, and raupō is native too. Where I could not find any New Zealand statement I marked the plant "not listed, check first" instead of guessing.

- Decided how to show the plants. I have no plant artwork, and a reader needs to actually recognise the plant, so I went with real photos from Wikimedia Commons. I only used photos with licences that allow it (CC0, public domain, CC BY, CC BY-SA), checked each licence and author through the Commons API, and recorded the credit for every photo so it can be shown on the page. Five of my first picks were not good enough when I looked at them (one was a microscope shot of duckweed, another was mostly muddy water) and I swapped them.

- Put the species list on the plants page as a preview, with a small photo and a New Zealand status tag next to each one. The full pages come when the database does.

Nothing blocking me.

The main challenge was legal, not technical. I did not expect so many normal pond plants to be banned in New Zealand, and once I knew, the site could not just recommend them. So each plant now has an honest New Zealand status, and the banned ones say why. It makes the section more useful here than a generic plant list would be.

The images took a couple of tries. Downloading from Wikimedia needed the right request format and a proper identifying user agent, and I could not judge a photo from its file name, so I made a contact sheet of all twenty and looked at them before accepting any.
